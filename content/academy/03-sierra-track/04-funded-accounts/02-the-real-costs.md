## The fee, the reset fee, the activation fee

An evaluation rarely costs one fee. The usual pieces are:

- **The evaluation fee.** Either a one-off payment or a monthly subscription that continues until you pass or cancel.
- **The reset fee.** If you break a rule, some firms let you pay to restart the evaluation instead of buying a new one. Many people reset more than once.
- **The activation fee.** Some firms charge a fee when you pass, to open the funded account. Others charge a monthly fee on the funded account instead, or both.

Terms vary from firm to firm and change often, so read the current terms in full, including the parts about fees after you pass.

## Adding it up

The cost that matters is the total you will have paid before your first payout, not the headline fee. Work it out with this worksheet:

| Item | How to count it |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluation fees | The monthly fee x the months you expect it to take, or the one-off fee |
| Resets | The reset fee x the number of resets you might need |
| Activation | The activation fee, if there is one |
| Funded-account fees | Any monthly fee after you pass |
| Platform and data | Every month of platform and data costs, from the first day of the evaluation |
| **Total before the first payout** | The sum of all of the above |

Be honest about the resets. If you have never traded under these rules before, assuming you will pass the first time is optimistic.

Then look at the payout rules: the share of profits you keep, any minimum balance or buffer before withdrawals are allowed, minimum trading days between payouts, and caps on how much can be withdrawn. Your total cost is only recovered once payouts under those rules add up to more than it.

## Data and platform costs on top

Some evaluations include a platform and market data. Others require you to pay for them separately, and some switch from included data during the evaluation to paid data on the funded account. Exchange data fees also depend on whether you count as a professional or non-professional user, which the exchange's agreements define.

Check exactly what is included at each stage, and add the rest to the worksheet.

## Published pass rates, and what they leave out

Some firms publish pass rates or payout figures. A single number can hide a lot, so ask:

- **Per what?** A pass rate per evaluation bought is very different from one per trader. One person who buys five evaluations and passes once is a 20% pass rate per evaluation.
- **What happens after passing?** How many funded accounts receive a first payout, and how many a second?
- **Payouts against fees.** Of the traders who received payouts, how many were paid more than they had spent in fees?
- **Over what period, and checked by whom?** A figure with no period and no independent check is marketing, not data.

A pass rate on its own says nothing about how many traders came out ahead.

> [!warning] Budget it as a cost you may lose
> An evaluation fee buys an attempt, not an income. Treat every fee as money you may not get back, and only spend what the income test allows.

> [!check] A firm says 20% of its evaluations pass. What does that leave out?
> - [ ] Nothing, since passing means the trader made money
> - [ ] Only the name of the platform the firm uses
> - [x] How many traders reach a payout, and whether their payouts exceed the fees they paid
> Passing is only one step, and the number that matters is payouts received against total fees paid.
