## An order is a request, not a trade

When you press buy, nothing has been bought yet. You have sent a **request**. A trade
happens only when that request meets someone willing to take the other side. That meeting
is called a **fill**.

Almost everything about how an order behaves comes down to one choice: which do you care
about more, **getting filled** or **the price you get**? You cannot fully have both.

## A market order puts getting filled first

A market order says: _fill me now, at the best price available._

- You will almost certainly be filled, and quickly.
- You do **not** control the price. You take whatever the other side is offering at that
  instant.

The gap between the price you expected and the price you got is called **slippage**. In a
busy market it is usually small. In a thin or fast-moving one it can be large.

## A limit order puts the price first

A limit order says: _fill me at this price or better, and not otherwise._

- You control the price completely. You will never get a worse one.
- You do **not** control whether you are filled. If the market never reaches your price,
  nothing happens, and you may watch it move away without you.

## The trade-off, side by side

|                       | Market order         | Limit order                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Will it fill?         | Almost always        | Only if your price is met    |
| Do you set the price? | No                   | Yes                          |
| The cost you accept   | Slippage             | Missing the trade altogether |

## What to take from this

Neither is better. They answer different questions. Before placing any order, it is worth
being able to say which of the two you are choosing to give up, and why.
