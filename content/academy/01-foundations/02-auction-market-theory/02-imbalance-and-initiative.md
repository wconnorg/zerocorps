## Imbalance

Balance lasts only while both sides are content. When one side becomes more eager than the other, the market is **out of balance**, or in **imbalance**.

In imbalance, price moves mostly in one direction. It is searching for the price where the other side will finally show up in enough size to trade. The signs on a chart are easy to recognise once you know them:

- Price travels further in each bar, and bars overlap less.
- Little time is spent at any one price.
- Pullbacks are shallow and short.

A strong move is simply the auction advertising: sellers wanted, higher up, or buyers wanted, lower down.

## Initiative versus responsive

Auction Market Theory sorts activity by where it happens compared with value.

- **Responsive** activity is buying below value or selling above value. These participants respond to prices they see as a good deal compared with where business has been done. Responsive activity pushes price back toward value.
- **Initiative** activity is buying above value or selling below value. These participants are willing to trade at prices the market has not accepted yet. Initiative activity pushes price away from value.

| | Above value | Below value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying | Initiative | Responsive |
| Selling | Responsive | Initiative |

Neither is good or bad. They describe two different motives: one side thinks the old value still holds, the other thinks value has moved.

## Who is in control, and how a profile shows it

In balance, responsive activity dominates. Buyers show up near the bottom, sellers near the top, and price keeps coming back to the middle. A profile of a balanced session is wide in the middle and thin at the edges.

In imbalance, initiative activity on one side dominates. A profile of that session is long and thin, because price kept moving and did not spend long anywhere.

Comparing one session's value with the previous session's is a useful description too:

- Value built **higher** than yesterday's describes buyers in control across the session.
- Value built **lower** describes sellers in control.
- Value **overlapping** yesterday's describes balance continuing.

## How balance turns into imbalance and back

Markets alternate between the two states, over and over:

1. **Balance.** Price rotates inside a range, and responsive activity holds the edges.
2. **The break.** Initiative participants push through an edge, and trade continues outside it.
3. **Imbalance.** Price moves in one direction, searching for the other side.
4. **The response.** The other side finally shows up in size, and two-sided trade returns.
5. **New balance.** A new range forms, with new value, and the cycle starts again.

Not every attempt at an edge becomes a break. Many fail, and price rotates back inside. The next lesson is about telling the difference, and why it only becomes clear with time.
