## Value is where business gets done

In the first chapter you saw the market as an auction: price moves up to find sellers and down to find buyers. Auction Market Theory adds a simple idea on top. Over any period, most of the trading happens in a fairly narrow band of prices. That band is called **value**.

Value is not a "fair price" in an economic sense, and nobody decides it. It is just where buyers and sellers were both willing to keep doing business, again and again. Prices far above it were too expensive for most buyers; prices far below it were too cheap for most sellers.

On a profile chart, which you will meet in the next chapter, value is usually drawn as the **value area**: the range holding about 70% of the period's trading.

## Balance

When both sides are broadly content, the market is **in balance**. Trade is two-sided and stays inside a range:

- Near the top of the range, sellers become more active and buyers less eager.
- Near the bottom, buyers become more active and sellers step back.

Markets spend a large share of their time in balance. A single session can be a balance, and several sessions can form one larger balance.

## Rotation inside balance

Movement from one side of a balance to the other is called a **rotation**. Price travels toward the top until sellers respond, then travels toward the bottom until buyers respond. Rotations happen on every timeframe, from a few minutes to many days.

Here is a description of one imaginary ES session. Price trades between 5,000 and 5,020 all day and touches each edge several times. Most of the hours are spent between 5,006 and 5,014.

- The range 5,000 to 5,020 is the **balance area**.
- The band 5,006 to 5,014 is where **value** built up.
- Each trip from one edge to the other is a **rotation**.

Notice that this describes what happened. It does not say what comes next.

## Time at a price is the sign of acceptance

The longer a market trades at a price, the more that price is **accepted**. Time is how the auction votes. A price visited for a few seconds was barely accepted at all. A price where the market spent two hours, trading back and forth, clearly was.

The next chapter gives you two tools that measure this directly: the TPO profile counts time at each price, and the volume profile counts contracts at each price.

| Term | What it describes |
| --- | --- |
| Value | The prices where most trading took place |
| Balance | Two-sided trade inside a range |
| Rotation | A move from one side of a balance to the other |
| Acceptance | Time and trade building up at a price |

> [!note] Why this matters
> Every tool in this course is a way of answering one question: is the market in balance, building value where it is, or is it moving away to find value somewhere else?

> [!check] A market spends four hours trading between two prices and touches each edge several times. What best describes it?
> - [ ] An imbalance, because price keeps changing direction
> - [ ] A trend that has not started yet
> - [x] Balance, with price rotating between the edges of an accepted range
> Two-sided trade inside a range, with time spent throughout it, is what balance means.
