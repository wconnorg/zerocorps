## Why a routine

A routine turns good intentions into habits. When the same steps happen in the same order every day, you stop relying on willpower in the moment, which is exactly when willpower is weakest. It also makes each day's record comparable with every other day's.

The routine has four parts: prepare, trade, review, and once a week, the numbers.

| When | What | Roughly how long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the session | The checklist | 15 to 30 minutes |
| During the session | The rules and nothing else | Your trading window |
| After the session | Review and journal | 15 to 30 minutes |
| Once a week | The numbers | About an hour |

## Pre-session checklist

- **Read the setup card** from top to bottom, and note the version you are trading.
- **Check the economic calendar** for scheduled releases during your window, and mark the no-trade times.
- **Mark the levels** your setup uses, such as the prior day's high and low.
- **Check the platform:** live data connected, not delayed; the simulated account selected; your chart template applied; the right contract month, especially near a rollover.
- **Know your size.** Confirm your fixed risk per trade, and work out the size for a typical stop so the arithmetic is not new when a trade appears.
- **Check yourself.** If you are exhausted, rushed or upset, it is fine to skip the day and write down why. Skipping a day is better than trading it badly.

## During the session: the rules and nothing else

- Take only the trades the card calls.
- Write the plan in the journal before you enter: entry, stop, target, size.
- Place the stop with the entry, every time. Move it only if a written rule says so.
- Take a screenshot at entry and at exit.
- If the card has a daily limit, such as stopping after two losses, stop when you reach it.

Anything else you notice, such as a pattern that is not on the card or an idea for a new filter, goes on a notepad for later. It does not get traded today.

## Post-session review

- Complete each journal row: exit, reason, result in R, rules followed, notes and screenshots.
- Log any trade the card called that you missed, with the result it would have had.
- Check each trade against the card: did you follow every rule? If not, which one did you break, and why?
- Write one sentence on what went well and one on what to work on.

## The weekly numbers

Once a week, update the numbers from your sim log: trades, win rate, average win and average loss, expectancy in R, the largest drawdown so far and the number of rule breaks. Put them next to the backtest's numbers, as you will do in full in the next lesson.

Rule breaks deserve as much attention as results. A week with a small loss and no rule breaks is a good week of practice.

> [!check] Mid-session, you notice a chart pattern that is not on your setup card. What does the routine say?
> - [ ] Take it on sim, since sim money is not real
> - [ ] Take it, but at half size
> - [x] Let it pass, and write it down as an idea for later
> During the session only the card's rules count, and new ideas are tested properly later, not traded on the spot.
