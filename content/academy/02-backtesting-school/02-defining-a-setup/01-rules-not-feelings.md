## You cannot test a feeling

A backtest counts what a set of rules produces on past data. If the rules are vague, every trade depends on how you read the chart that day, and on history you can already see. The numbers you end up with describe your judgement on a good day with the answers visible, not a setup you could repeat.

So before any testing, the setup has to be written down as rules. That is the whole job of this chapter.

## A rule is something a stranger could follow

A good rule can be checked on the chart with a yes or a no. A vague one leaves room to argue.

- **Vague:** "Buy when price looks strong at support."
- **Clear:** "On the 5-minute MES chart, during the regular session, if price trades below the prior day's low and a 5-minute bar then closes back above it, buy at the open of the next bar."

Every part of the clear version can be checked. Which chart? Which session? Which level? What exactly has to happen? When exactly do you enter?

Watch for words that feel precise but are not: *strong, clean, big, sharp, near, looks like, momentum*. Each one needs a definition you can measure. "A big bar" becomes "a bar with a range of at least 8 points". "Near the level" becomes "within 2 ticks of the level".

> [!note] An example, not a recommendation
> The prior-day-low example runs through this chapter only to show what clear rules look like. It is not a setup this Academy recommends, and nothing here says it has an edge.

## Discretionary versus mechanical

Setups sit somewhere between two ends.

| | Mechanical | Discretionary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions | All defined in advance | Some left to judgement |
| Two people, same chart | Take the same trades | May take different trades |
| Backtesting | Straightforward | Much harder to do honestly |
| Adapting to conditions | Only as far as the rules say | Yes, if the judgement is good |

Discretion is not wrong, but it has to have limits you can write down. "Skip the trade if a major scheduled economic release is due within 15 minutes" is a rule, even though it is a filter. "Skip the trade if it feels off" is not.

The practical path is to start fully mechanical. Once you have a tested set of rules and a record, you will know exactly where judgement might help, and you can test that too.

## The test

Here is the test for any rule set: **would someone else take the same trades from the same rules?**

Hand your rules to another person with a stretch of chart and ask them to mark every trade. Compare their marks with yours. Wherever you disagree, a word in your rules was read two ways. Define it, and try again.

If nobody is available, you can run the test on yourself. Mark a week of charts, put your marks away for a few days, then mark the same week again from the rules alone and compare. You will be surprised how often you disagree with yourself.

> [!check] Which of these is a rule that a stranger could follow?
> - [ ] Enter when momentum looks strong
> - [x] Enter at the open of the next bar after a 5-minute close above the prior day's high
> - [ ] Enter when the setup feels clean
> Every part of it can be checked on the chart with a yes or a no, so two people would mark the same entry.
